Research

Secure and efficient computing systems

My research develops system-on-chip co-design methods that treat security and privacy as first-class constraints alongside performance, energy, accuracy, and implementation cost.

Hardware-aware machine-learning security

I investigate security vulnerabilities that emerge when machine-learning algorithms are mapped onto physical hardware. This includes backdoor attacks enabled by memory faults, device behavior, and accelerator-specific execution paths.

Side-channel analysis

I study practical side channels across devices and modalities, including power, electromagnetic emanations, and shared system-level caches. My work considers both supervised and unsupervised transfer across hardware platforms.

Secure accelerator co-design

I explore the relationship between algorithm mapping, microarchitecture, circuit design, and attack surface, with an emphasis on end-to-end methods for secure and efficient ML deployment.

In-memory computing

I analyze energy–accuracy–security trade-offs in resistive in-memory computing and develop methods for understanding how circuit non-idealities affect both inference quality and security.
