Area 01

Energy-efficient eNVM in-memory computing accelerator design

This work develops and analyzes energy-efficient hardware for machine learning, including in-memory computing and accelerator architectures. It considers the relationships among circuit non-idealities, energy, accuracy, performance, and security.

  1. IMC benchmarking

    OJ-SSCS 2022CICC 2022

    An open benchmarking resource for in-memory-computing architectures with indexed chip data, normalized metrics, and reproducible plots.

    • 106indexed papers
    • 144operating points
    • 7years of reports

    Paper·Website·Code

  2. Crossbar IMC analysis

    ISCAS 2022

    Computational-accuracy limits of resistive crossbar in-memory computing across MRAM, ReRAM, and FeFET device models.

    • <1 ppResNet-20 accuracy gap to an exhaustive search
    • 6–7 bminimum ADC precision at N = 512

    Paper·Website·Code

  3. Parallel-bar IMC analysis

    JxCDC 2024

    Energy-accuracy trade-offs in resistive parallel-bar in-memory computing, modeling how device, array, sensing, and ADC non-idealities jointly limit SNDR.

    • 3eNVM technologies: MRAM, ReRAM, FeFET
    • 22 nmMRAM measurements validate the model

    Paper·Website·Code

  4. SEC–MRAM IMC

    JSSC 2024ESSCIRC 2023

    Behavioral modeling, circuit design, and measured 22 nm MRAM in-memory-computing silicon with statistical error compensation.

    • 2.7–6 dBmeasured SNDR improvement from SEC
    • 5×energy reduction at iso-SNDR
    • 22 nmmeasured MRAM prototype

    Paper·Website·Code

Area 02

Security vulnerabilities of ML accelerators

This work in hardware-aware machine-learning security includes side-channel analysis, fault-based attacks, backdoors, and secure accelerator design. It examines how vulnerabilities emerge when learning algorithms are deployed on physical systems.

  1. ROBBIN

    ICCAD 2026

    Rowhammer-based backdoor injection during neural-network inference, connecting device-specific memory faults with attacker-chosen model behavior.

    • ≈90%attack success on three DDR4 chips
    • 64–73%fewer pages than Don’t Knock

    arXiv·Website·Code

  2. SLAC

    CCS 2026

    Access-driven CPU-to-GPU side-channel attacks through the system-level cache on Apple silicon.

    • >90%GNN edges recovered
    • 94.8%peak input recovery
    • 4,096shared-cache sets mapped

    arXiv·Website·Code

  3. Bridges both areas

    EAS-IMC

    TCAD 2026IEDM 2024ICCAD 2024

    Energy, accuracy, and security trade-offs in embedded non-volatile in-memory computing across MRAM, ReRAM, and FeFET technologies.

    • 0.1%accuracy gap to the fixed-point baseline
    • 4.18 dBlowest SNDR with near-baseline model recovery
    • 3query-based model extraction attacks

    Paper·Website·Code

  4. Attack from Shadows

    AsiaCCS 2026

    Unsupervised side-channel transfer learning across devices, leakage modalities, and hardware implementations.

    • 61traces to key recovery, FPGA power → MCU power
    • 435traces to key recovery, MCU power → x86 cache

    Paper·Website·Code

  5. HOTSPOT

    TCAS-I 2026

    On-chip differential temperature sensing for detecting localized power anomalies from hardware Trojans on a measured 65-nm CMOS prototype.

    • 95%peak Trojan detection accuracy
    • 6.65×higher SNR than supply-current sensing at 1.95 µW
    • 65 nmmeasured CMOS prototype

    Paper·Website·Code
